# Pitching an EDM single beyond Spotify editorial

Research for issue #16 (map: #14, Release Assistant v1). Researched 2026-11-13.

**Question.** Beyond Spotify editorial, where can an early-stage independent EDM producer ($0-100 per release) pitch a single, and how? For each channel: cost, typical response rate, lead time, and what a good pitch contains.

**Confidence key.** Each claim is tagged:
- **[Primary]**: the platform's or label's own docs, help pages or published figures.
- **[Secondary]**: a named practitioner or trade-press write-up (dated where possible).
- **[Folklore]**: common advice with no measured source. Use it as a default, not a fact.
- **[Synthesis]**: my own inference from the above, for the playbook.

---

## 1. The policy line first: what Spotify forbids

- Spotify's User Guidelines prohibit "selling a user account or playlist, or otherwise accepting or offering to accept any compensation, financial or otherwise" and "artificially increasing play counts or follow counts, artificially promoting Content… (ii) providing or accepting any form of compensation (financial or otherwise)". **[Primary]** https://www.spotify.com/legal/user-guidelines
- Spotify for Artists: "Paid 3rd-party services that guarantee streams aren't legitimate". Services that sell guaranteed playlist placement for money break its terms. Penalties include withheld royalties, corrected stream counts, removal from playlists, removal from Spotify, and per-track fees charged to labels and distributors for very high rates of artificial streams. **[Primary]** https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/third-party-services-that-guarantee-streams/
- Musosoup tells its own curators that "Spotify's Terms and Conditions strictly prohibit charging artists for playlist placement". Every offer must include a no-cost playlist placement, and paid extras can never be a condition of it. **[Primary]** https://curator-support.musosoup.com/tips/6VpFeCpHCvYtpJ56DeUGjH/how-promos-and-bundles-work-with-spotify-playlists/6VpFeCpHCtTv32scoMBcJh
- SubmitHub's line is that paying for placement, or paying a curator more for an accept than for a decline, is payola. Requiring someone to keep streaming a playlist to stay on it, and bot-play followed by a fee request, are also illegitimate. **[Primary, SubmitHub's own position]** https://www.submithub.com/story/payola-and-spotify

**POLICY FLAGS for the app (hard rules for drafts and playbook):**
1. **Never** suggest or draft a payment offer to a playlist curator, a "placement fee", or any service that guarantees streams, plays, followers or placements. This is a clear violation.
2. **Gray zone (flag, don't ban):** SubmitHub, Groover and Playlist Push pay curators to *listen and respond*, not to *place*. Accept and decline pay the same. The platforms present this as compliant. Spotify has never publicly endorsed or condemned this model, and its guideline wording ("any compensation, financial or otherwise") is broad. **[Synthesis]** Keep the tool's language as "paying for a review", never "buying a playlist".
3. Any curator who asks for money *after* accepting, or charges extra for Spotify playlist inclusion, is a red flag. Also flag any playlist with a sudden follower spike and no engagement, though that is **[Folklore]** with no Spotify-published heuristic.
4. Paid placement on YouTube channels, blogs, TikTok/IG influencers or DJ pools is not covered by Spotify's rules. Be careful when it drives traffic to Spotify via bots. **[Synthesis]**

## 2. Curator platforms (paid review)

### SubmitHub
- **Cost:** Premium credits are $1 each, falling to about $0.78-0.80 in bulk. A curator asks 1-5 credits. Standard (free) credits give 2 every 4 hours, max 2 held, and curators have no obligation to respond to them. **[Primary]** https://www.submithub.com/help/QPHgFWDSCm82W7vLJ , https://www.submithub.com/help/7rf247chNWHh6kP23
- **Response:** On a premium submission the curator must respond within 72 h, listen at least 60 s, and either approve or write at least 20 words of feedback. Otherwise the credit is refunded. **[Primary]** https://www.submithub.com/help/QPHgFWDSCm82W7vLJ , https://www.submithub.com/story/how-we-doubled-approval-rates-on-submithub
- **Approval rate:** About 10% (2015-2020), now about 25% platform-wide on premium (post-Aug 2023 changes). There is no genre breakdown. Each curator's approval % is shown, based on their last 500 premium decisions. **[Primary]** https://www.submithub.com/story/how-we-doubled-approval-rates-on-submithub , https://www.submithub.com/help/bdD2ueWchDRt8ELNt
- **Lead time:** You can submit before release (private SoundCloud, MP3 or unlisted YouTube). Curators wait for the release date, and SubmitHub notifies them when links are updated. Some curators take only unreleased tracks, others only released ones. **[Primary]** https://www.submithub.com/help/W28MDKa5GGjnBkDxX
- **Advice:** Start with 5-10 curators, then re-target **[Primary]** https://www.submithub.com/help/7rf247chNWHh6kP23 . Space singles about a month apart to avoid curator fatigue **[Primary]** https://www.submithub.com/help/FH7uXLRm293ioE8oY
- Covers Spotify playlisters, blogs, YouTube channels, and paid "influencers" (TikTok/IG, premium only, paid per share). **[Primary]** https://www.submithub.com/help/QPHgFWDSCm82W7vLJ

### Groover
- **Cost:** 1 Grooviz = €1. A standard curator or pro costs 2 Grooviz, "Top" ones 4-6. Packs start at €47 with up to 20% off, and Grooviz don't expire. **[Primary]** https://help.groover.co/en/articles/2966389-do-i-have-to-pay-to-use-groover , https://groover.co/lp/pricing
- **Response:** Feedback guaranteed within 7 days or the Grooviz is refunded. The answer rate is stated as ">85%" (FAQ) or "90%" (homepage); the figures conflict. "Most reply in <72h". **[Primary]** same URLs.
- **Acceptance rate:** No platform-wide figure is published. Badge definitions imply the norms: "High acceptance rate" means more than 15% shared, and "selective" means under 4%. **[Primary]** https://help.groover.co/en/articles/4705388 . A realistic expectation is single digits to about 15% per curator. **[Synthesis]**
- **Who:** 1,500+ vetted curators and pros: blogs, radio, playlisters, YouTube channels, labels, bookers, managers. DJs are not a named category. **[Primary]** https://help.groover.co/en/articles/4705388
- Feedback must be at least 15 words. Groover keeps 1 of the 2 Grooviz. **[Primary]** https://help.groover.co/en/articles/2966389-do-i-have-to-pay-to-use-groover

### Musosoup
- **Cost:** Submitting is free. If approved, you pay a campaign fee of £42 from 1 Jan 2026 (was £36). Curators then make offers, which can be free or paid. **[Primary]** https://musosoup.com/blog/campaign-fees-are-changing-from-january-2026 , https://musosoup.com/artists-how-it-works
- **Lead time:** Campaigns can start up to 90 days before release. **[Primary]** https://musosoup.com/artists-how-it-works
- Spotify playlist placement must be offered at no cost (see §1). **[Primary]**

### Playlist Push
- **Cost:** Spotify campaigns start at $280, average $550. **Outside the $0-100 budget.** **[Primary]** https://help.playlistpush.com/en/articles/1888782-how-much-does-a-campaign-cost

## 3. Playlist curators direct (free)
- **Cost:** $0. **Response rate:** no measured source. It is widely reported to be low for cold email, and higher for genre-niche curators who list a contact. **[Folklore]**
- What works per platform docs: target genre fit, one track, short note. **[Folklore]** generalised from SubmitHub/Groover guidance above.
- Musosoup and SubmitHub curators often run the same playlists. Many EDM curators publish a submission form or email in the playlist description. **[Folklore]**

## 4. YouTube promo channels
- Big EDM channels (examples from 2021: MrSuicideSheep via its own submit form, UKF via ukf.com/submit-promo, Monstercat Uncaged via email, WaveMusic via SubmitHub). Links and subscriber counts may be stale, so re-verify. **[Secondary, dated May 2021]** https://www.hypebot.com/best-youyube-promotion-channels-for-submitting-your-music.md
- Proximity (2020 interview): accepts "maybe 1/500" submissions. Wants unique, well-produced tracks and a clear artist story. Social numbers are "not important at all". Pet peeves: unfinished tracks, repeated messages, CC'ing channel lists. **[Secondary, dated Jul 2020]** https://gravitascreate.com/proximity-interview/
- Practical route for small artists: channels listed on SubmitHub/Groover (YouTube channels are a named Groover category). **[Primary]** https://help.groover.co/en/articles/4705388
- **Watch for:** some channels claim Content ID on uploads via their CMS. Proximity itself complains of false claims. Check who monetises before agreeing. **[Secondary]** same interview.

## 5. Blogs / press
- Electronic outlets such as Magnetic Magazine take submissions via a form: private streaming link, release date, bio, artwork, press photos, and background. Premieres are one format. No fees or turnaround are published (paid "promotional packages" exist). **[Primary]** https://magneticmag.com/submit-your-music-to-magnetic-magazine/
- Most small EDM blogs are reached via SubmitHub, Groover or Musosoup (above).
- **Lead time:** pitch premieres 2-4 weeks before release. **[Folklore]**. No outlet publishes a number. SubmitHub and Musosoup support pre-release pitching (up to 90 days on Musosoup).

## 6. DJ promo pools and DJs
- **Digital DJ Pool:** artists upload to an independent catalog (600K+ DJs signed up, 75+ tracks/day). Tracks are tagged with BPM and key, and downloads drive charting. **[Primary]** https://digitaldjpool.com/MusicPromotion . Uploader subscription $10/month for up to 10 tracks; paid boosts $10 (24 h chart/trending) and $70 (ReSpin). One artist's best track reached dozens of DJs and 312 downloads. Upload Friday mornings, set the key accurately, remixes and house/trance do best. **[Secondary, B. Hazard, Dec 2024]** https://www.hypebot.com/how-digital-dj-pools-benefit-artists-insights-and-tips-brian-hazard.md
- **Proton promo pool:** free for labels and DJs. Promos go live on the Beatport pre-order date and stay up for 2 weeks after release. Schedule pre-orders 1 week minimum, ideally 28 days. DJs leave feedback. **Label-side tool:** a self-releasing artist needs a label account. **[Primary]** https://intercom.help/proton-radio/en/articles/4229133-proton-s-promo-pool-how-it-works
- **Direct-to-DJ promo (email or Inflyte-style):** build your own targeted list from DJs' charts and the labels they play. Ask permission first, use one address per contact, prune non-openers, segment by genre, and keep the blurb to a few sentences. **[Primary, Inflyte's own blog]** https://plus.inflyteapp.com/8-ways-to-increase-your-promo-hit-rate/ . Inflyte publishes no open or download rates.

## 7. Labels (for a later signing)
- **LabelRadar** (Beatport-owned): free for labels. Artists get 5 free credits a month (only when the balance is 0), and one submission costs 5 credits, so effectively **1 free label submission a month**. PRO adds 70 credits a month (price not on page). If a label doesn't listen within 30 days, the credits are refunded. Anjunabeats, Armada, FiXT and others are on it. **[Primary]** https://help.labelradar.com/what-are-credits , https://help.labelradar.com/can-i-use-labelradar-for-free , https://help.labelradar.com/is-labelradar-free-for-labels-and-promoters
- **Armada:** "Drop Your Demo" button on site. Finished, preferably mastered track. Private SoundCloud link (no attachments). Intro under 10 sentences: who you are, achievements, the track, why it fits the label. No bootlegs. Paid guaranteed feedback exists via Armada University. No response time is stated. **[Primary]** https://www.armadamusic.com/news/how-to-submit-your-demo
- **Spinnin' Talent Pool:** public SoundCloud track, one a week, community voting chart. The label has up to 3 months to decide. The terms reference an option on management and publishing, so read them before uploading. Current activity is unconfirmed. **[Primary]** https://www.spinninrecords.com/talentpool/faq
- **General demo etiquette (Toolroom):** one label per email, no CC. Send your single strongest track and mention you have more. Keep it short, use a streaming link (not WeTransfer), and name brief credentials. Research label fit. If no reply, wait about a week and send a *different* demo rather than chasing. **[Secondary, label-run academy]** https://toolroomacademy.com/the-dos-and-donts-of-demo-submissions
- Labels usually want **unreleased** music. Pitching a single you've already self-released is mainly for relationship building. **[Folklore]**, though widely consistent; Armada's "can't be released" wording on bootlegs points the same way.

## 9. Playbook implications for a $0-100 single **[Synthesis]**

1. Budget split example: ~$40-60 on SubmitHub premium and/or Groover, targeting 15-25 *genre-exact* curators picked by approval %, plus $10 on Digital DJ Pool. Keep the rest for a second wave after the first feedback.
2. Free every release: one LabelRadar submission (an unreleased track, ideally the *next* single), plus direct emails to 5-10 niche curators and blogs.
3. Timeline: start label pitching for the *next* track while promoting this one. Pitch curators and blogs 2-3 weeks before release (pre-release links). Upload to the DJ pool on release Friday.
4. Pitch-draft template elements (shared by Armada, Toolroom, Magnetic, Inflyte, Proximity): one track, a private streaming link, release date, 2-4 sentence bio with one concrete credential, why it fits *this* outlet (name a track they've supported), artwork and press photo link, BPM and key for DJs. No attachments, no CC, no mass blasts. One polite follow-up, or for labels a different demo.
5. App guardrails: block "pay for placement" wording, warn on guaranteed-stream services, and label paid-review platforms as "paid review, not placement".
