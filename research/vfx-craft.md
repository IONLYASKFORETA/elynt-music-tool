# What makes music visuals look hand-made by a VFX artist?

Research for issue #9, part of the Visualizer map (#4). Researched 2026-11-13.

**Question.** How do professional visual artists who work on electronic music (music videos, festival LED content, VJ loops, TouchDesigner/Notch work) make visuals that feel intentional and made for one track, and not like generic "reactive" output?

**How to read this.** Each claim cites the interview, breakdown, tool doc or paper it comes from. Claims marked **[inference]** are my synthesis across sources and are not stated by any one source. The sources quoted most are first-person interviews (Max Cooper, Ryoji Ikeda, Tarik Barri, Weirdcore, Boxcat, Desilence, defasten, Pauric Freeman, the V Squared Labs team behind Amon Tobin's ISAM), project write-ups from Notch and Derivative, the TouchDesigner docs, Michel Chion's film-sound theory, and one peer-reviewed study on music and color.

---

## Short answer

Hand-made visuals start from **one concept per track**. The concept is then expressed through a **small, constrained visual vocabulary**, arranged across the **track's macro arc**. The visuals sync **selectively**, at a few meaningful points. Cheap visualizers do the reverse: every element twitches to every band of the spectrum at the same intensity from the first bar to the last, using recognizable stock effects.

---

## 1. Concept first, then form

- **One idea per piece.** Max Cooper: "The concept for the project came first, which then spawned many visual and musical ideas." He sometimes sent the concept to a visual artist and then scored the music to their drafts ([Resolume blog, "Maxing Out on Science & Art"](https://www.resolume.com/blog/16844)). For *One Hundred Billion Sparks*/*Yearning for the Infinite*, each chapter's video was "about" one idea, for example "how you can get infinity by constantly branching structures" ([Ableton blog](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/composing-infinity-max-cooper-his-new-album/)).
- **Don't illustrate the song literally.** Optical Animal (JR Scola and Max Nova) aim for the visual concept "to breathe new perspective into the songs performed, rather than being an on-the-nose interpretation." Jeremy Thompson (2n Design) said the aim was to keep any narrative from calcifying "into an overt story feeling" ([Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/514655/video-jockey-concert-interviews/)).
- **Mapping without musicality fails.** Cooper: "if you try to map data to music you invariably make a non-musical mess" ([Resolume](https://www.resolume.com/blog/16844)). The mapping has to serve the feeling, not the other way round.
- **Each set gets its own visual language.** Boxcat Studio give each artist a concept with "different visual pillars throughout each set," and make content "for the space it is designed" ([EDM Identity](https://edmidentity.com/2024/01/28/boxcat-mas-tiempo-drumsheds-interview/)). The ISAM team: "The show visuals were designed per track with an overarching, loose narrative hemming it all together" ([Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/visualizing-amon-tobins-isam-v-squared-team/60785)).
- **Tuning by feel.** Tarik Barri (Thom Yorke) plays with visuals "until it feels like some sort of a symbiotic relationship is appearing… it's really just about if it 'feels right'" ([Crack Magazine](https://crackmagazine.net/article/long-reads/tarik-barri-on-his-live-visuals-for-thom-yorkes-upcoming-solo-tour/2)).

**For the Visualizer [inference]:** the **vision** step in #4 matches what professionals do. A vision should be **one sentence-level concept plus a constrained vocabulary** (a single motif family, a palette and a motion character), not a list of effects.

## 2. Structure across a track: arc, motifs, escalation, restraint

- **A macro arc of intensity.** Cooper's *Emergence* was built to fit "a live show arc of increasing musical intensity" ([Resolume](https://www.resolume.com/blog/16844)). The Dom Dolla and John Summit Coachella 2024 content (Notch) followed "a visual arc… transitioning from the simplest visuals to increasingly complex ones," "a progression that mirrored the passage of the music" ([Notch](https://www.notch.one/madewithnotch/dom-dolla-john-summit-coachella24)).
- **Contrast between minimal and maximal sections.** The same Notch project contrasted "the intense visual complexity of the maximalist sections" with "percussively reactive minimalist elements," aiming for "a harmonious relationship between minimalism and maximalism" ([Notch](https://www.notch.one/madewithnotch/dom-dolla-john-summit-coachella24)).
- **Chapters, then layering.** Cooper describes the show having "whole chapters of one or the other [abstract vs. human footage], but later on in the show it starts to layer the two together" ([Ableton](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/composing-infinity-max-cooper-his-new-album/)). A motif is introduced on its own and later combined with others.
- **Journey through worlds.** Jeremy Thompson: "the show's structure had us moving from one 3D world to another, like a ride" ([Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/514655/video-jockey-concert-interviews/)).
- **Don't overplay a gimmick.** The ISAM team "were also careful to not over-do it and risk losing the interest factor" with their Kinect segment. They also chose varied themes "to keep things moving" ([Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/visualizing-amon-tobins-isam-v-squared-team/60785)).
- **Negative space.** Boxcat: "Making use of the negative space" creates intimacy and "thematic moments." Their style is "much cleaner and more minimal" than the norm ([EDM Identity](https://edmidentity.com/2024/01/28/boxcat-mas-tiempo-drumsheds-interview/)).

**Gap.** None of the sources I fetched give a beat-by-beat recipe for building into a drop. **[inference]** Their shared pattern implies: hold back complexity and contrast in intros and breakdowns, so the drop has headroom; reveal the most complex form at the drop; and bring motifs back changed (not new ones) for the second drop. Chion's synch-point idea (section 4) explains why the downbeat of a drop works best as one large, rare synchronisation event rather than as more twitching.

## 3. Choosing color and form from emotion

- **Emotion drives music-to-color associations, consistently across cultures.** In Palmer et al. (UC Berkeley, PNAS 2013), faster music in a major key was paired with lighter, more saturated, yellower colors, and slower music in a minor key with darker, greyer, bluer ones. The researchers could predict "with 95 percent accuracy how happy or sad the colors people pick will be." Results from the US and Mexico were very similar, and angry faces were linked to dark reds ([ScienceDaily summary of the PNAS paper](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/05/130516151256.htm); paper: [PMC3670360](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3670360)).
  - Use as a default mapping: **energy/valence → lightness and saturation; happy ↔ warm yellow, sad ↔ dull blue-grey, angry ↔ dark red.**
- **A deliberately narrow palette is a signature.** Ryoji Ikeda works in black and white, aiming for something "as universal and transparent as mathematical equations"; "I tried to be as minimal as possible" ([FACT](https://factmag.com/2023/04/13/ryoji-ikeda-interview)). Roy Bennett (NIN lighting): uplights in "full spectrum but don't use amber" ([Live Design](https://www.livedesignonline.com/concerts/nine-lives-nine-inch-nails)). MxC's look is deliberately "high-contrast and high-saturation," combining neon, chrome and illusion art ([Notch blog](https://medium.com/notchblog/mxc-on-keeping-things-maximal-with-a-minimal-team-6a4a32a432d3)). Desilence found a new palette that defined their aesthetic ([Notch blog](https://medium.com/notchblog/an-interview-with-desilence-418bbdeae4c9)).
- **Form from simple systems.** Cooper prefers "simple systems" that "contain a huge amount of visual richness," with "the artistry not coming from the human animator so much as from nature itself" ([Ableton](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/composing-infinity-max-cooper-his-new-album/)). For a mathematical piece, "we chose to minimize and simplify the visual form to its basics" ([Resolume](https://www.resolume.com/blog/16844)).
- **Imperfection makes it feel human.** Barri: tech art can be "super precise, very linear, and often black and white," but music's beauty "lies in imperfections… ever so slightly off" ([Crack](https://crackmagazine.net/article/long-reads/tarik-barri-on-his-live-visuals-for-thom-yorkes-upcoming-solo-tour/2)). Cooper: "Music without imperfection is sterile" ([RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/11/max-cooper-interview)). Weirdcore: "I love accidents and mistakes" ([The Quietus](https://stage1.thequietus.com/?p=25150)).

## 4. What to sync to, and what to ignore

- **Synchresis.** Chion defines it as "the mental fusion between a sound and a visual when these occur at exactly the same time." Viewers will fuse almost any sound to an image if the timing is exact ([FilmSound.org, Chion](https://filmsound.org/chion/sync.htm)). So tight timing on a few events matters more than literal resemblance.
- **Synch points are salient, punctuating moments**, not continuous coupling. A point of synchronization is "a salient moment, that needs to be perceived as such," and it often reinforces an "effect of meaning." In Chion's *Le Million* example, a sound builds until its peak lands on the visual ([USC Audiovisualities, Chion](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/audiovisualities/point-of-synchronization)). **[inference]** If everything is a synch point, nothing is: reserve hard sync for downbeats, drops, impacts and signature hits.
- **Pick the signal on purpose.** Pauric Freeman: "Generating graphics with an audio waveform vs a gate signal will have very different results… it's about designing the experience and carefully selecting data." He also warns that "there is a limit and, beyond that, you can lose the believability" ([Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/pauric-freeman-working-sound-image-input-devices-and-human-perception/65814)).
- **Sync to instruments, not to the master mix.** Beeple found it much easier to read sound visually when "I looked at the wave form of just ONE instrument at a time." He built each loop's visuals per instrument and layered them, with a constant background "drone" layer ([CDM, Beeple interview](https://cdm.link/instrumental-video-for-instrumental-music-interview-with-beeple/)). Scott Pagano says strong relationships need "a bit of data manipulation" rather than raw input ([Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/touchdesigner-and-scott-pagano/60812)). **For the Visualizer:** stems, or kick/snare/band separation from the mixdown, are worth more than a single loudness value.
- **Connect, don't mimic.** Barri: "It doesn't mimic the sound, it's more about connecting to it" ([Crack](https://crackmagazine.net/article/long-reads/tarik-barri-on-his-live-visuals-for-thom-yorkes-upcoming-solo-tour/2)).
- **Pre-rendered timelines for the hero moments.** On ISAM, real-time "curtain songs" sat next to fully pre-rendered songs driven by a central show timeline ([Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/visualizing-amon-tobins-isam-v-squared-team/60785)). An offline renderer can do the same: plan the whole track ahead with knowledge of what comes next, for example so a build ramps up and lands exactly on the drop.
- **Typical tool features.** TouchDesigner's Audio Analysis component outputs Low/Mid/High band levels, spectral centroid, slow and fast partial density, and kick, snare and rhythm detection. Each has threshold and smoothing controls, and the docs advise activating "as few of the quantities as you need" ([Derivative docs](https://derivative.ca/UserGuide/Palette:audioAnalysis)). A typical direct mapping is volume to size and pitch to colour, or bass to fluid and highs to particles ([Interactive & Immersive HQ](https://interactiveimmersive.io/blog/technology/audio-reactive-visuals-and-how-to-use-them/)). Pros treat these as raw material to shape, not as finished output.
- **What to leave unsynced [inference].** Across the sources, the background, palette and camera move at the speed of a section or phrase, not of a beat. Hi-hats and other fast transients are rarely mapped to anything large. Weirdcore time-stretches clips to "super slow… with loads of motion blur" for an "organic feel" ([The Quietus](https://stage1.thequietus.com/?p=25150)). Slow motion against fast music is a deliberate choice.

## 5. Common abstract and minimal techniques (seen in sources)

- Simple generative/natural systems: branching, growth, particles, lines ([Cooper, Ableton](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/composing-infinity-max-cooper-his-new-album/)). Line-based feedback systems and oscilloscope/Lissajous figures ([Benjamin Heim, Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/benjamin-heim-forging-intimate-connections-between-sound-visuals/60687)).
- Monochrome data/grid minimalism with perfect sync ([Ikeda, FACT](https://factmag.com/2023/04/13/ryoji-ikeda-interview)). "Flatness" of the image, at Thom Yorke's request ([CDM on Tarik Barri](https://cdm.link/morphing-abstractions-to-new-thom-yorke-superband-tarik-barris-poetry-of-flatness/)).
- Negative space and minimal organic looks ([Boxcat](https://edmidentity.com/2024/01/28/boxcat-mas-tiempo-drumsheds-interview/)).
- Time-stretching and motion blur on loops ([Weirdcore, Quietus](https://stage1.thequietus.com/?p=25150)).
- Layering with alpha, a constant background bed plus per-instrument layers ([Beeple, CDM](https://cdm.link/instrumental-video-for-instrumental-music-interview-with-beeple/)).
- Point clouds and particle "paint strokes," treating the tool "like live painting" ([Desilence, Notch blog](https://medium.com/notchblog/an-interview-with-desilence-418bbdeae4c9)).
- Simplifying: defasten "often realise[s] it doesn't need to be so complex… Sometimes, it's better to simplify" ([Notch blog](https://medium.com/notchblog/defasten-on-the-evolution-of-his-craft-dca769a04c37)).

## 6. Tells that make automated visualizers look cheap

Sourced:
- **Recognizable stock filters and tutorial looks.** Desilence: "You try to avoid people recognising specific filters"; "We don't want to be easy to deconstruct"; leaning on tutorials means "everything will look a bit the same" ([Notch blog](https://medium.com/notchblog/an-interview-with-desilence-418bbdeae4c9)).
- **Excess motion.** Boxcat: the norm is people wanting "to go crazy with heavy animations and crazy camera movements"; they go cleaner ([EDM Identity](https://edmidentity.com/2024/01/28/boxcat-mas-tiempo-drumsheds-interview/)).
- **Data mapped without musical intent** produces "a non-musical mess" ([Cooper, Resolume](https://www.resolume.com/blog/16844)).
- **Over-pushed mapping** loses "believability" ([Freeman, Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/pauric-freeman-working-sound-image-input-devices-and-human-perception/65814)).
- **Sterile precision**, with no imperfection ([Barri](https://crackmagazine.net/article/long-reads/tarik-barri-on-his-live-visuals-for-thom-yorkes-upcoming-solo-tour/2); [Cooper, RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/11/max-cooper-interview)).
- **Overusing one gimmick** ([ISAM team](https://derivative.ca/community-post/visualizing-amon-tobins-isam-v-squared-team/60785)).

[inference], following from the above:
- Everything reacts to everything: no hierarchy, no synch-point punctuation (Chion).
- Same intensity from bar 1 to the end: no arc, so the drop has no headroom.
- Raw, unsmoothed FFT jitter on large elements, and reactions to hi-hats.
- Generic rainbow/HSV-cycling color unrelated to the track's mood (contrast Palmer et al.).
- Reaction without anticipation: visuals respond after the hit, whereas an offline render can build into it.
- Bars, waveforms and circular spectrums as the main image, centered and static in composition.

## Implications for the Visualizer (not decisions, inputs for #4)

1. The **vision** = concept sentence + motif family + palette (seeded from emotion → lightness/saturation/hue) + motion character.
2. Each **section** gets an intensity budget along the track arc; breakdowns spend little, drops spend most.
3. Reactivity is **tiered**. A few hard synch points (downbeats, drop, signature hits). Medium coupling for kick/snare on chosen layers. Slow, smoothed coupling (band energy, spectral centroid) for color and camera. Hats and other fast transients are mostly ignored.
4. Prefer stems or separated features to master loudness.
5. Use offline lookahead to build into events.
6. Add deliberate imperfection, and avoid stock-looking effects.
